Diffusion and drift

Diffusion is due to a concentration gradient. Drift is the movement of carriers under the electric field in the depletion region.

Equilibrium

At equilibrium, diffusion current and drift current are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Net current is zero.

1.6 • DEVICE
A one-way current device

A PN junction diode is a two-terminal semiconductor device. It allows current to flow readily in one direction and opposes current in the other direction.

ANODE (A)

Connected to the P-type region.

CATHODE (K)

Connected to the N-type region. The diode symbol's bar marks the cathode.

APPLICATIONS

Rectification, switching, signal detection and circuit protection.

Silicon diode

Typical forward cut-in voltage is approximately 0.7 V at room temperature.

Germanium diode

Typical forward cut-in voltage is approximately 0.3 V at room temperature.

1.7 • BIASING
Forward bias

Battery positive is connected to P and negative to N. The depletion layer narrows and the barrier decreases.

DEPLETION WIDTH

Narrower than at equilibrium.

CURRENT

A significant current flows when the applied voltage is sufficient.

Conventional current flows from P-side (anode) to N-side (cathode).

Forward bias connection

Battery + → P-side (anode); battery − → N-side (cathode). Barrier decreases.

Reverse bias connection

Battery + → N-side (cathode); battery − → P-side (anode). Barrier and depletion width increase.

The V-I graph shows how diode current changes with the voltage applied across it.

FORWARD REGION

Current is small at first, then rises rapidly after the cut-in (knee) voltage.

REVERSE REGION

A small reverse saturation current flows and remains nearly constant until breakdown.

BREAKDOWN

Beyond breakdown voltage, reverse current rises sharply. A current-limiting resistor is essential.

1.9 • ZENER DIODE
Designed for reverse breakdown

A Zener diode is a specially designed PN junction diode intended to operate in its reverse breakdown region.

FORWARD BIAS

It behaves similarly to an ordinary PN junction diode.

REVERSE BIAS

At the Zener voltage Vz, reverse current increases sharply while voltage remains approximately constant.

MAIN USE

Voltage regulation and overvoltage protection.

The Zener must remain within its specified current and power ratings. The series resistor limits current.

1.9 • APPLICATION
Nearly constant output

The Zener diode is reverse-biased and connected in parallel with the load. A series resistor is connected between the supply and the output node.

HOW IT REGULATES

When input voltage rises, Zener current increases. When input voltage falls, Zener current decreases, helping maintain output voltage.

LOAD CHANGE

When load current changes, Zener current adjusts to compensate, provided the diode remains in breakdown.

FORMULAE

Is = (Vin − Vz) / Rs
IL = Vz / RL
Iz = Is − IL

Regulation is active when Zener current is positive and within its safe rating.

Series resistor (Rs)

Limits the current supplied to the parallel combination and protects the Zener diode.

Load resistor (RL)

The output voltage is measured across the load. In regulation, it is approximately equal to Vz.
